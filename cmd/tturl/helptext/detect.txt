tturl detect adaptively searches candidate requests for one early or late
arrival-order outlier. It compares subsets and stops when it finds an outlier,
rules out the configured minimum effect, or spends the comparison budget.

Use tturl measure when every request needs the same fixed experiment. Use
tturl detect to search a larger candidate set for one suspected outlier.

## State the direction

'--direction' late searches for a relatively late request. '--direction' early
searches for a relatively early one. '--direction' either searches both
directions against supplied baselines and requires a reserved strategy. The
accepted aliases fast=early and slow=late describe arrival order, not elapsed
service time. JSON uses canonical directions; raw argv retains the submitted
spelling. Choose the scope before collecting data.

## Understand the model

This is a specialised one-result outlier decision, not a general ranking
method. It returns at most one candidate. Its guarantees concern conditional
mean rank under adaptive subset selection and random position assignment.

Before running tturl detect:

- Build the candidates from a defensible common reference population.
- Choose the searched direction before observing the adaptive evidence.
- Use a different design for systematic peer differences or multiple extremes.

Normalised rank divides rank by width minus one: first is 0 and last is 1. A
late search uses that value; an early search uses one minus it.

Without baseline controls, evidence is relative to the peers scheduled in each
comparison. Given the past and the wave's planned peer set, each non-target
candidate's normalised rank must have conditional mean no greater than 0.5 in
the searched direction. A qualifying target for a negative decision must
sustain a searched-direction conditional mean of at least 0.5 plus the
configured edge in every scheduled peer set.

A block marked '--baseline-only' supplies reusable controls rather than
candidates. '--baseline-supply' N sets each expanded template's simultaneous
per-comparison capacity; 'unlimited' permits as many instances as the selected
width can use. The default is one copy per expanded request per comparison.
Supply requires '--baseline-only'; unlimited supply permits only one baseline
block. Baseline blocks exclude '--warmup-only' and reject '--repeat',
including inherited explicit values, even one.

'--strategy' selects peer-first, rolling-peer-first, baseline-confirmed,
rolling-baseline-confirmed, baseline-reserved, rolling-baseline-reserved, or
auto. The default auto strategy resolves as follows:

  direction   baselines  resolved strategy
  early/late  none       peer-first
  early/late  supplied   baseline-reserved
  either      supplied   baseline-reserved

An either-direction search requires at least one baseline. Peer First and
Baseline Confirmed accept only a known early or late direction.

Peer First spends every broad-field position on candidates. It introduces
available baselines only after eliminations free positions. This gives broad
candidate coverage, but its early evidence remains peer-relative. Multiple
same-direction targets can mask one another, and opposing extremes can make an
ordinary peer appear extreme. Supplying a baseline does not repair evidence
already collected against peers.

Baseline Confirmed also discovers broadly against peers, then requires clean
baseline-relative evidence before a positive commit. It trades extra
confirmation work for a baseline-relative positive claim. Its nominations and
negative evidence still include peer-relative comparisons, so several targets
can still mask a candidate or invalidate a negative premise.

Baseline Reserved includes controls from the first comparison. Its evidence is
baseline-relative throughout, isolating candidates from multiple target
extremes at the cost of fewer candidate positions per comparison. It supports
early, late, and either-direction searches.

Rolling Baseline Reserved uses the same clean-reference allocation but keeps a
bounded window of comparisons in flight. It buffers physical completions,
commits the maximal contiguous dispatch prefix, adapts once, and refills the
window. Selection is explicit; auto selects atomic-wave Baseline Reserved.

The rolling guarantee has a stronger dependence premise. A non-target's
baseline-relative conditional mean bound must hold given every lower dispatch-
indexed outcome, including outcomes from physically overlapping comparisons.
Arbitrary dependence among in-flight comparisons is not allowed. All rolling
strategies update ordered results sooner under their stronger ordered-prefix
conditional-mean premise and are never auto-selected. They are not an
equivalent performance mode. Use tturl measure or tturl analyse to choose
connections and pacing that retain the timing signal; that experiment does not
establish this conditional premise.

One representative baseline appearance is sufficient for the statistical
reference when its conditional-mean premise is defensible. Several appearances
can give more informative control-relative ranks at wider widths. Baselines
are not known targets: positive controls and bracketing both arrival extremes
require different nulls and are outside this feature.

The selected solver cannot verify that a baseline or peer population satisfies
its premise. A negative result also depends on the configured minimum edge. It
assumes neither independent comparisons nor a parametric rank distribution.
Candidates must still be comparable under the stated direction and model.

## Comparisons, width, and allocation

One comparison is one batch send of candidates and any selected baseline
instances. '--width' N sets the requested comparison width. Without
baselines it is capped at the candidate count. With baselines it is capped at
the candidate count plus finite aggregate supply; unlimited supply can fill
the requested width. The default is 2. With baselines, at least one candidate
is required; without them, at least two are required. The effective width
stays fixed after resolution, and eliminated peers can fill comparison
positions. tturl detect has no trials, cycles, arrangement, capture or
extraction flags. Wider comparisons cover more candidates or provide finer
baseline-relative ranks, but change target concurrency and can change the
observed signal.

For example, this explicit width-eight design compares eight requests at once:

  tturl detect -k --direction late --width 8 --comparisons-max 200 \
    --repeat 8 'https://127.0.0.1:8443/sleep?duration=100us'

Request blocks expand URLs and '--vary' values before roles and supply are
applied. A baseline block's supply therefore belongs independently to every
expanded template. Finite control allocation visits each template in canonical
expansion order before taking a second instance from any template, then
repeats that layered pass while supply remains. Unlimited allocation cycles
through the templates in the same order.

Configured aggregate supply is preserved even when a comparison cannot use all
of it. The effective baseline cap is at most effective width minus one because
every comparison contains a candidate. Reports show configured supply and this
separate cap. Supply is simultaneous capacity per comparison and is reusable
in later comparisons; it is not a run-wide request budget. Three expanded
templates with supply two offer six reusable copies per comparison; at most
width minus one fit beside a candidate. For example:

  tturl detect --direction either --width 3 --comparisons-max 200 \
    --block https://example.com/candidate \
    --block https://example.com/control --baseline-only --baseline-supply 2

Baseline blocks reject '--repeat'; '--baseline-supply' sets reusable capacity.

tturl reports requested and effective width when '--width' is explicit.
Confirm a surprising negative from a wider design with '--width' 2.

'--padding' and '--padding-at' define a position-indexed padding schedule for
evidence-bearing comparisons. The schedule is resolved once against the
effective width and remains fixed as candidates are eliminated and filler
peers are reused. Candidate identities are assigned uniformly to positions
before padding is applied. Padding is a recorded heuristic delivery condition;
it does not remove position bias or change the guarantee. See:

  tturl help padding

As evidence accumulates, the search focuses on unresolved candidates and drops
those it can rule out. Candidates therefore receive different sample counts. A
sample is one comparison in which a candidate participated while still in
contention. A ruled-out candidate may later be reused only to fill a batch.
That reuse counts as a request participation, not another sample. Physical
work includes baseline and filler positions in rank-complete comparisons;
failed comparison operations are reported separately. Inferential samples
include only completed comparisons while each candidate was in contention.
Physical and inferential means are descriptive across changing opponents,
rather than calibrated effect estimates.

'--connections' sets the maximum number of concurrent comparisons directly.
'--connections-fit' selects HTTP/2 PING fitting with a finite pacing limit.
Rates alone pace work with up to one connection.

The resulting concurrency sets the largest adaptive wave. Every comparison in
a wave is planned before the wave starts, and all its outcomes are combined
before the next adaptive choice. More concurrency can reduce wall-clock time
but increases target contention. Recheck a marginal result with less
concurrency.

'--batch-rate-max' also caps comparison starts across the connection pool.
It is a maximum offered rate, not promised throughput. For fitting assumptions
and operational cautions, see:

  tturl help delivery

## Set error risks, edge, and budget

'--false-positive-risk' P bounds the prospective family-wise risk of falsely
naming any candidate as an outlier. '--false-negative-risk' P separately
bounds the prospective risk of ruling out a qualifying outlier. The defaults
are 0.01 and 0.05, respectively, and both must lie strictly in (0, 1). Naming
a request usually carries the greater practical cost, so the default asks for
stronger positive evidence without tightening the negative boundary. These are
repeated-run error bounds under the stated premise, not posterior
probabilities that this result is right or wrong, and they do not form one
joint bound.

The defaults apply to one decision. For a campaign making at most N decisions
with a campaign false-positive budget A, setting each false-positive risk to
A/N is a conservative allocation that does not assume independent decisions.
Automated campaigns should set both risks explicitly.

'--edge' EDGE is the positive, dimensionless minimum target advantage covered
by elimination's false-negative guarantee. The default is 0.02 and the value
must lie strictly in (0, 0.5). Positive evidence adapts independently of edge:
a smaller effect can still produce a found result.

For ordinary normalised arrival rank R, first is 0 and last is 1:

  Search  Directional score  Example mean R  Apparent edge
  late    R                  0.8             0.8 - 0.5 = 0.3
  early   1 - R              0.2             0.8 - 0.5 = 0.3

Raw mean rank can exceed one in wider batches; the edge uses the normalised
scale. With '--edge' 0.2, the false-negative guarantee applies to a target
sustaining a directional conditional mean of at least 0.7 under the stated
premise. A target with mean 0.6 falls outside that guarantee, although it can
still be found. A smaller edge covers subtler targets but usually costs more
comparisons; a decision within the budget is not guaranteed.

Peer-relative tturl detect requires the target to sustain the advantage in
each adaptively scheduled reference set under its conditional-mean premise.
Baseline-relative evidence uses the fraction of selected controls arriving
before the candidate for late, reversed for early, rather than necessarily its
rank among every request in the comparison. An aggregate average from tturl
measure does not establish the required conditional bound.

'--comparisons-max' N caps solver-recorded comparisons. The default is 50,000.
At the default width, that permits at most 100,000 measured request
participations. A finite limit N permits at most N times the effective width;
an explicit width of eight permits at most 400,000 participations under the
default limit. Reaching a finite limit returns an inconclusive result with
successful completion. It is not a negative finding.

A limit of one deterministically ends this two-candidate search inconclusive:

  tturl detect -k --direction late --comparisons-max 1 \
    --block 'https://127.0.0.1:8443/sleep?duration=100us' --name first \
    --block 'https://127.0.0.1:8443/sleep?duration=100us' --name second

Use '--comparisons-max' unlimited only as an explicit choice to continue until
a decision or interruption:

  tturl detect -k --direction late --comparisons-max unlimited \
    --block 'https://127.0.0.1:8443/sleep?duration=100us' --name first \
    --block 'https://127.0.0.1:8443/sleep?duration=5000us' --name second

tturl records a failed comparison without retry because the target may already
have processed part of its request batch.

Account for failed work in the target-exposure budget. See:

  tturl help design

## Prime each adaptive connection

'--warmup' N makes N priming attempts on every connection before that
connection contributes comparisons. tturl detect requires at least one
distinct request block marked '--warmup-only'; it never chooses candidates as
priming work. Each attempt sends that batch in authored order and is excluded
from solver evidence.

Workers prime independently, so a ready connection can proceed without a run-
wide barrier. A connection-fatal priming failure permits one replacement for
that worker, and the replacement repeats the complete sequence. A second fatal
failure or replacement acquisition failure makes the decision unavailable. A
stream-scoped incomplete priming batch consumes its attempt.

The Experiment section reports initial priming request operations and the
maximum with one replacement per worker. Execution reports each attempt,
connection identity, incomplete attempt, acquisition failure, and replacement.
Priming obeys the same request and batch pacing as comparisons. See:

  tturl help delivery

## Bound response work

'--response-body-max' SIZE caps accepted response DATA per stream. The default
is 8 MiB. '--batch-timeout' DURATION caps transport time for each priming or
comparison batch attempt. The default is 30s. Both also accept unlimited.

Crossing either local limit makes the result unavailable. tturl discards the
affected connection and stops the run without retrying, priming a replacement,
or admitting ranks from the incomplete adaptive wave. The Experiment section
records both resolved limits. JSON failure details use stable codes
response_body_limit and batch_timeout.

For example, this deliberately small body limit demonstrates an unavailable
result when either response exceeds four accepted DATA bytes:

  tturl detect -k --direction late --response-body-max 4B \
    --block 'https://127.0.0.1:8443/' --name first \
    --block 'https://127.0.0.1:8443/missing' --name second

For shared units, timeout boundaries, and transport semantics, see:

  tturl help delivery

## Read the outcome

The report has one of four states:

  found         evidence names one early or late arrival-order outlier
  negative      no matching outlier detected at the configured minimum edge
  inconclusive  the comparison limit ended before either decision
  unavailable   acquisition stopped or failed without a valid decision

A found result controls the family-wise false-positive risk under the reported
premise. A negative result controls its false-negative risk only for the
configured direction, edge, and premise. It does not prove equality or exclude
a weaker effect. Inconclusive and unavailable results carry no guarantee and
do not show that an outlier is absent or that the field is otherwise uniform.

For a found result, positive log evidence reaches the reported commit
threshold in natural-log units. The configured '--edge' does not enter that
positive evidence. A negative result reports the normalised-rank edge it
excludes.

Exit status is separate from outcome. Found, negative, and finite-budget
inconclusive results exit zero when reporting succeeds. Interrupting tturl
detect before a decision writes an unavailable result with stopped completion
and exits one, for both bounded and unbounded searches. Other unavailable
results and operational failures also exit one. Usage errors exit two.

A downstream reader that closes a stdout pipe is a consumer-controlled stop,
not a detect decision; it exits zero and may omit the terminal result.

Scripts must inspect the reported outcome and completion state.

The candidate table is exploratory. It sorts sampled requests by observed mean
rank. Candidates may have met different peers during different adaptive
phases, so their means do not form a like-for-like ranking.

Text shows four rows from each end by default. '--rank-rows' N changes that
count. '--rank-rows' all shows every sampled candidate. '--pin' PIN retains a
matching label or named fan in a bounded view. Unsampled candidates appear
separately. These options affect presentation only.

## Structured adaptive evidence

tturl detect uses tturl.detect/v1. A complete stream contains one run,
every candidate, baseline template and warmup request, and one terminal result.
Request records provide dense global request_id values and their roles. Each
baseline's supply_limit is a positive integer or null for unlimited supply.

The run's plan groups requested and effective width, comparison_limit,
padding, warmup and initial/maximum priming request operations. Acquisition
records response limits, resolved delivery and in_flight_comparison_limit. The
sibling method records its ID, requested/resolved strategy, canonical
direction, false_positive_risk, false_negative_risk and edge. The method ID
identifies allocation, evidence, scheduling, position randomisation and
stopping rules.

Result detection.state is available or unavailable. Available detection has a
finding with status found, negative or inconclusive and the commit threshold
in natural-log units. Found identifies a candidate request_id and its observed
direction. Found and negative carry the ranking method's actual error-risk
guarantee. Negative also records excluded_edge, equal to method.edge.
Unavailable detection retains legitimate partial evidence; completion.failure
records the runtime cause.

Each detection.requests entry joins the catalogue by request_id. Its physical
facet records participations and raw/normalised mean rank. Candidate
inference, when available, records samples, means, positive/elimination log
evidence and elimination status. An eliminated candidate reused as filler can
have more physical participations than inferential samples:

  {"request_id": 0,
   "physical": {"participations": 12, "mean_rank": 1.25,
                "normalised_mean_rank": 0.625},
   "inference": {"samples": 5, "mean_rank": 1.4,
                 "normalised_mean_rank": 0.7, ...},
   "status_counts": [...], ...}

Physical participations across candidates and baselines equal execution's
retained request operations. Baselines have physical evidence; solver samples
belong to candidates. status_varied and non_2xx_observed attach to each
request. status_distributions_differ compares participating requests across
both roles. Priming and failure entries use worker_id and connection_id.
Comparison failures preserve explicit request_id/position assignments and a
common failure object.

tturl detect drains response bodies and hashes every body byte received, while
retaining no body prefixes, response header maps, or trailers. Every decoded
header section remains subject to the shared 1 MiB limit in 'tturl help
output'.

For shared framing, schema, joins, and partial-stream handling, see:

  tturl help json

## Examples

### Known-direction zero-or-one search

Use the default Peer First strategy when the field has at most one late target
and its peers form a defensible reference population:

  tturl detect -k --direction late --comparisons-max 200 \
    --block 'https://127.0.0.1:8443/sleep?duration=100us' --repeat 2 \
    --name reference \
    --block 'https://127.0.0.1:8443/sleep?duration=5000us' --name candidate

Inspect the reported premise as well as the finding. The result neither
estimates the time gap nor identifies its cause.

### Known direction with several possible targets

Use a varied candidate block and a representative early baseline when several
candidates could be late. Auto selects Baseline Reserved:

  tturl detect -k --direction late --width 4 --comparisons-max 200 \
    --block 'https://127.0.0.1:8443/sleep?duration=D' \
    --vary 'D={100us,5ms,6ms}' --name candidate \
    --block 'https://127.0.0.1:8443/sleep?duration=100us' \
    --baseline-only --baseline-supply unlimited --name reference

The reference must represent the conditional mean boundary for every
candidate; matching one candidate's duration does not establish that property
by itself.

### Either-direction search

Search both extremes only against a representative baseline:

  tturl detect -k --direction either --width 3 --comparisons-max 200 \
    --block 'https://127.0.0.1:8443/sleep?duration=10us' --name candidate-a \
    --block 'https://127.0.0.1:8443/sleep?duration=5000us' --name candidate-b \
    --block 'https://127.0.0.1:8443/sleep?duration=1000us' \
    --baseline-only --baseline-supply unlimited --name reference

Inspect whether the found direction and response facts fit the intended
comparison. Allocate both error risks across a campaign of repeated searches.
